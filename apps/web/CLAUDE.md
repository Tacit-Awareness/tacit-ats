# @tacit-ats/web

Next.js (App Router): the client area, where members of a client org sign in and work on their own jobs and pipeline.

Where members of a client org sign in and work: Phase 1 of tacit-internal's `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-13-ats-parity-design.md`. Dormant until configured: `/client/**` answers 404 unless `WORKOS_CLIENT_ID`, `WORKOS_API_KEY`, `WORKOS_COOKIE_PASSWORD` (32+ characters), `SUPABASE_URL` and `SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY` are all set (`lib/client/config.ts`; the one-time WorkOS and Supabase steps are in this repo's `.env.example`).

- **One session, under one prefix** (`proxy.ts`, which matches only `/client` and `/client/**`; `/` redirects to `/client`). Clients sign in with WorkOS AuthKit (`wos-session`). Until 2026-10-14 this area lived inside tacit-internal's `apps/internal`, split by path from the staff session; it moved here so the client product and Tacit's workspace no longer share an app, a domain or a session.
- **`lib/client/session.ts`**: `resolveClient()` builds a per-request database client from the user's own WorkOS access token (`createUserClient`, role `authenticated`) and asks the database who that is (`current_actor_id()`), so the page and the policies agree by construction. Not bound yet means a first sign-in: `lib/client/bind.ts` matches the verified email to the actor an admin invited, inside the token's org (`@tacit-ats/core`'s `bindClientActor`). Nobody is created on sign-in. `requireClient()` is the throwing form, for actions.
- **Sign-ins that start at WorkOS.** Accepting a WorkOS invitation (or signing up from its email) runs AuthKit's hosted flow and then lands on `/client/callback` without the PKCE cookie our own sign-in sets, so AuthKit cannot verify it and refuses (`CallbackError` `missing_pkce_cookie` / `missing_auth_params` / `oauth_state_mismatch`). The account exists by then; the callback's `onError` (`lib/client/callback-error.ts`) answers with a "Your account is ready, continue" page linking to `/client`, which signs in from our side. Any other failure is a plain "Couldn't sign in, try again" page, never the error itself. A link, not a redirect, so a browser that drops the cookie cannot loop. (Until 2026-10-14 this was AuthKit's bare JSON error, the first thing an invited client saw.)
- **`lib/client/bind.ts` is the only file in the client area that may touch the service role**, and it returns a verdict, never the client.
- **`src/client-area.test.ts`** enforces the rules above from the source: the import walk (nothing reachable from `app/client` or `lib/client` may name a privileged client, except `lib/client/bind.ts`), and that every server action starts with `await requireClient()`.
- **Pages so far**: `/client` (Home: the Recruiting Overview below for admins and the hiring team; for a limited interviewer, the page it always was: the people they interview, the jobs they can see, the team), `/client/jobs` (Job Postings since 2026-10-29, below) and `/client/jobs/[id]` (2026-10-13: the job and its pipeline on the shared `components/pipeline-board.tsx`, plus "Add a candidate"). Everything is read through RLS; nothing is filtered in the page. A refused sign-in renders `app/client/refusal.tsx` on every page.
- **The job board** reads with core's `getRolePipeline` on the member's own client, so the cards are exactly the applications they may read (an external recruiter sees only their own adds; an interviewer gets a pointer to `/client` instead of a board). The edit controls appear only when `can_on_role(job, 'can_edit_pipeline')` says so, and "Add a candidate" also needs `can_add_people()`. The writes (`jobs/[id]/actions.ts`: add, move, edit stage/owner/next action, log a touch, remove) are core's pipeline functions on the same client, so the policies decide again. Adding goes through core's `addCandidateToRole`: an email already on a person the member can see reuses that person, otherwise a people row is made in the org; a person in the org the member cannot see is not revealed, so they get a second row. The action checks `can_edit_pipeline` before creating anyone, so a refused add leaves no orphan person. Notices and errors are codes (`jobs/messages.ts`). Core never touches the Tacit funnel for a client org's application (`advancePersonFunnel` returns for any org but Tacit's), and `getRolePipeline`'s `companyName` is empty for a member who may not read `companies` (everyone but an admin). A card's name opens the candidate page (`/client/candidates/[id]`). Its header links the job's post (`/client/jobs/[id]/post`).
- **Where a client org starts**: tacit-internal's ATS Admin (`/admin/orgs` in its `apps/internal`, staff admins only), with `@tacit/core`'s `createClientOrg` and `inviteOrgMember` on the service role. Nothing in this repo creates an org or its first admin; a first sign-in here only binds the actor that page invited.
- **The team and its invitations** (2026-10-14 as `/client/team`; on `/client/interviewers` since 2026-10-30, `/client/team` only redirects there for old links, like `/client/calendar`): under the interviewers, the org's members for admins and the hiring team; an admin also sees who is invited and not signed in yet, and invites, re-sends and withdraws (`interviewers/team.tsx`, `interviewers/actions.ts`, codes in `interviewers/messages.ts`). An interviewer (`limited`) gets the pointer home, whose page lists the team, and any notice from an action, since an admin who steps down to interviewer lands there. No service role: the actor is written by the `invite_org_member` definer function on the admin's own token (tacit-internal's packages/db, `20261014120000_client_invites`), which checks the caller is an admin and writes only into the org on that token, so the form never names an org. WorkOS then sends the email (`lib/client/workos.ts`, AuthKit's own `getWorkOS()` client, with the inviter named), and a first invitation WorkOS refuses is withdrawn again. Withdrawing deletes the unbound actor and revokes WorkOS's pending invitation for the address, best-effort: an accepted email with no actor behind it binds nobody. `@tacit-ats/core`'s `client-auth/invite.ts` holds the sequence. `ClientContext` now carries the token's WorkOS organization and user ids for this.
- **Member access level** (2026-10-30; the prototype has no such control, so the picker follows the invite form's three levels): on the Members list an admin gets a select per member listed (`interviewers/access-form.tsx`), with Save appearing once it differs and a browser confirm when the row is their own, since they cannot undo stepping down. `setAccessAction` runs `lib/client/member-access.ts`'s `changeMemberAccess`, clientTools' audited `setMemberAccess` on the member's own token through `toolCtx`, the connector's `set_member_access` with source `dashboard`: `audit_log` gets `member.access_change` with the level before and after, and the same level again leaves no row. `set_org_member_access` decides: caller an admin, member in their org, and at least one other admin who has signed in, under a per-org lock, so two admins lowering each other leave one and an invited admin does not count. The only admin's row shows their level and "only admin" instead of the select (`soleAdmin`, pure, only a hint), and a stale page gets `last_admin`. The list is every page of `org_members` (`readMembers`, 1000 a read to 5,000; one read stopped at the database's 1000 rows and hid whoever sorted last, a second admin included); a list that stops short says so and names no only admin. A Tacit submitter (`tacit_agency`) is listed with a select too, since `org_members` cannot tell them apart and only `org_pending_invites` leaves them out: raising one does nothing they can use, and they never count as the admin left. Pending invitations are not editable here: withdraw and invite again. Each select is keyed by the render's time, so it comes back from any action showing the saved level. `revalidatePath("/client", "layout")`, because the sidebar names the level. Unit tests in `member-access.test.ts` (each refusal to its code, a bad level refused before the tool) and `interviewers/actions.test.ts` (the action by hand: tampered ids and levels, only a code in the URL, a database failure); `member-access.integration.test.ts` on real tokens: the audit row, the last admin, an invited, archived or Tacit admin not counting, stepping down and being refused on the next try, two and four admins at once, one change sent five times, the hourly limit, the hiring team and interviewers refused, another company not found, 1500 members read.
- **`/client/pipeline`** (2026-10-29; design/tats's Pipeline, the sidebar's **Routing & Tracking**): every open job's live applications on one board. `lib/client/pipeline.ts`'s `readPipeline` reads on the member's own token: each open job through core's `getRolePipeline` (not `listOpenPipelines`, which reads `companies(name)`, admin-only for a client member, and fails for everyone else), the non-cancelled `interviews` of every live application (150 ids a read: the `interviewing` columns need them, and so does knowing where a move into `interviewing` lands), and `can_on_role(job, 'can_edit_pipeline')` per job (`candidate-edits.ts`'s `editableRoles`). The columns are **computed, not stored** (`lib/client/pipeline-columns.ts`, pure, unit-tested): New (sourced, contacted), Screening, Scheduling (interviewing, nothing booked or held), Interview booked (a scheduled interview not yet ended), Your call (interviews held, a `scheduled` one past its end counting as held, nothing booked next), Offer; hired / rejected / withdrawn are left off. Department tabs (`?department=`, the department filter below), job tabs (`?job=`, only the department's open jobs once one is picked), a name filter (`?q=`, a GET form), a dozen cards a column until "Show all" (`?all=<column>`). A card is the person, the job (the headline once one job is picked), one status line (relative times only, "Interview in 2d", so no time zone is needed) red when core calls it stalled, and the owner; it opens the candidate page, where the rest of the edit controls are (and on the job's board). **Moving a card** (2026-10-30; `pipeline/board.tsx`, design/tats's board drag and `statusMenu`): by drag, by the Move button on the card (always shown on a touch screen, where a drag does not work), or by keys, only on a job the member may edit; a read-only card has none of them. Because a column is computed, it is not a stage: `lib/client/pipeline-moves.ts`'s `movesFor` (pure, unit-tested) lists a card's real stage changes from the candidate page's rules (`MOVABLE_STAGES`, `nextStage`), and a column takes a drop only when a stage, with this card's interviews, lands it in that column. So at most one of Scheduling / Interview booked / Your call takes a given card (the one its interviews say), moving between those three is never offered, and a drop on a column with no stage for the card moves nothing and says why (`NO_DROP`). A drop on New means `contacted`, the later of its two stages (anyone past it has been contacted, and this does not put them back in Applicant Tickets, which are `sourced`); sourced to contacted, inside New, is the menu or A. Hired has no column, so it is only ever a deliberate pick from the menu, A on an Offer card opening the menu instead. Rejecting needs a reason, so the menu's last line goes to the candidate page. `pipeline/actions.ts`'s `moveCardAction` is that page's `moveCandidate` (the audited `clientTools.updateStage`, source `dashboard`, the stage the board showed as `expectedStage`, the 300-an-hour limit), answering instead of redirecting; `parseBoardMove` refuses a close. The card moves at once (`useOptimistic`, `board-state.ts`'s `shiftCard`, pure, unit-tested), a refusal puts it back and says why in a toast that stays until dismissed, and keys are off while a move is in flight. Any refusal reads the board again, since it means the board was out of date. So does a write that throws, which says only that it can't tell (`unsure`, not `failed`): core's `moveStage` writes the stage and then its event, so the failure can come after the stage changed. The stage menu's keys (`lib/keys.ts`'s `menuKey`, pure, unit-tested) are page keys and act only with focus inside it, and it closes when focus leaves: a digit is a write, and F, / and ⌘K take focus to a field and `?` to a sheet. Focus follows a moved card unless someone has started typing in a field. A move changes the stage and nothing else: it does not cancel an interview, so a card with one booked can be moved out of `interviewing`, as on the candidate page. A column cancels `dragenter` as well as `dragover`, because crossing into a child fires only `dragenter` and the browser decides the drop on it; and a moved card remounts, so its `dragend` never arrives and `drop` clears the drag itself. An interviewer gets the same pointer home as on a job page. `pipeline.integration.test.ts` runs the read against the local stack: an admin's columns, a hiring manager seeing only their job (and its interviews), another company never; and the moves: a drop landing in its column, a card back from Offer landing by its interviews, hired leaving the board, a stale board refused, a hiring manager on their job and not another, another company's application, the audit row. `pipeline-moves.integration.test.ts` calls the move as each kind of member could by hand (admin, recruiter, coordinator, external recruiter, interviewer, a member off the team, another company's admin), checks the board offers moves to exactly those it would let through, and covers ten copies of a move at once, different moves at once, a hire filling an opening, and the hourly limit. `actions.test.ts` covers what the action says and re-reads.

Each is its own audit row and one that changes nothing leaves none; nothing is atomic, so they stop at the first refusal, the actions answer with a code and how far they got (not a redirect: the editor keeps what was typed), and the next save carries on from there, with the same job and post (`carryOn`). The rules take out what the database cannot store and a step cannot hold (NUL, half a surrogate pair, control characters), so those cannot stop a new role halfway; a body past Next's 1 MB is refused in the page. **Two editors**: the action compares the `updated_at` the page opened with the post's, so a save over someone else's comes back `changed` (before anything is written, status included), with "Save mine over it" (and the publish or close it was doing) or reload. The department and steps are the job's, which that version does not cover, so the page also sends them as it opened them (`seenJob`): one the member did not change is not written, so saving a post never puts back what another manager or the connector changed, and one they did change over someone else's is `changed` too. **Leaving**: unsaved changes ask first, on closing the tab (`beforeunload`) and on a link in the app (a capture listener, `leavesPage`, since `Link` fires no `beforeunload`); the shell's G keys go round it. **Status**: the chip is the post's; Publish / Reopen and Close post save first and then move it, and live needs the job open, so they are off on a job that is not (nothing on the web opens a job yet: `update_job` on the connector). The bar stays on screen (`Page`'s `stickyBar`, from a tablet up). A live post changes for applicants on Save.
